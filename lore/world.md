# The Lands of Ashenmoor

## The Sundering

No one alive remembers a time before the Grey Veil. The histories — those few that survived the burnings — speak of an age when the sky was blue and the sun could be seen directly, not filtered through the permanent overcast that blankets the world. The scholars of Hollow's Gate call it *The Sundering*: a cataclysm roughly 400 years ago in which something ruptured the barrier between the physical world and whatever lies beneath it.

What emerged was not demons or gods. It was **essence** — a raw, invisible current of power that saturated the soil, the water, and the air itself. Stone began to hum. Animals mutated over generations. And humans, slowly, began to change too.

The old civilizations collapsed within two generations. Not from the essence itself, but from the wars fought over it.

## The Current Age: Year 412 Post-Sundering

### How People Live

The world is not post-apocalyptic — it is post-*reconstruction*. Civilization has rebuilt itself, but in a fundamentally different shape. The old nations are gone. In their place stands a patchwork of **city-states**, **trade settlements**, and **frontier camps**, connected by dangerous trade routes that cut through untamed wilderness.

Most people live in one of three conditions:

**1. The Walled Settlements**
The majority of humanity huddles inside fortified towns. Life here is stable but stratified. You have merchants, craftsmen, laborers, and the ever-present Garrison — the armed force that keeps the walls intact and the wildlife outside. People eat, drink, work, and die within sight of the same stone walls their grandparents built. Rents are high. Food is adequate but monotonous. Entertainment is cheap alcohol and pit fighting.

**2. The Frontier**
Beyond the walls, scattered across the wilderness, exist small settlements of 20-100 people: miners, loggers, herbalists, and other extractors. These communities provide the raw materials that keep the walled towns running. Life here is dangerous, free, and often short. A frontier camp near the **Whispering Woods** might thrive for years — until the local fauna shifts migration patterns and overruns it overnight. Frontier folk are either very competent or very dead.

**3. The Drifters**
A growing underclass of people with no settlement allegiance. Some are exiles. Some are mercenaries. Many are treasure seekers who have heard rumors of pre-Sundering ruins containing artifacts worth more than a lifetime of honest labor. The player characters are Drifters — people who have chosen to stake their survival on their own nerve rather than submit to the rigid hierarchies of walled life.

## Why People Are the Way They Are

### The Essence Problem

Essence is simultaneously the greatest resource and the greatest hazard. It pools in certain geological formations — particularly near mineral deposits and ancient root networks. This means **mining** and **logging** are not just trades; they are the backbone of civilization AND the most dangerous professions that exist.

Raw essence infuses materials with latent properties. Iron ore pulled from an essence-rich vein is fundamentally different from surface iron — it's denser, harder, and carries trace magical potential. A red herb grown in essence-saturated soil can heal wounds. A ruby from a deep vein can store and discharge arcane energy. This is not magic in the fairy-tale sense. It is **material science** that the inhabitants of Ashenmoor have learned through centuries of trial, error, and death.

This creates a **deeply pragmatic culture**. People do not philosophize about essence — they use it or they avoid it. Superstition and empiricism exist side by side. A miner might pray to a forgotten saint before descending into a shaft, and then methodically test the ore density with calibrated weights on the way back up.

### The Essence Sickness

Prolonged, unprotected exposure to raw essence causes **Greying** — a progressive condition where the body begins absorbing ambient essence faster than it can metabolize. Early symptoms include insomnia, heightened senses, and euphoria (this is why some people become addicted to deep-zone exploration). Late-stage Greying causes tissue crystallization, psychosis, and eventually death. The body literally petrifies from the inside out.

This is why expeditions into the deep wilderness are terrifying. It's not just the creatures. The *land itself* is poisoning you.

## The Socio-Economic Reality

### Currency: The Coin

The universal currency is simply called **the Coin** — a small disc of pressed copper alloy stamped with the seal of the Merchant's Accord, a loose confederation of the major trading settlements. The Coin works because the Accord controls the supply. Counterfeiting is punished by exile into the wild — which is effectively a death sentence.

Coins have real value because they represent **guaranteed access** to the three things everyone needs:
- **Healing** — Medical treatment is expensive and monopolized by the Accord's licensed Therapists. Getting slashed by a beast is free. Getting stitched up costs coin.
- **Storage** — Stash space within walled settlements is limited and taxed. The more you hoard, the more you pay. Space is a luxury.
- **Equipment** — Weapons and armor are manufactured by Guild-licensed smiths. Quality gear requires essence-infused materials, which makes it extraordinarily expensive.

### Who Thrives

**The Merchant's Accord** — The closest thing to a government. They control the coin supply, regulate trade routes, and license the Therapists. They are not beloved, but they are necessary. Every transaction enriches them.

**Therapists** — Licensed healers who operate out of settlement clinics. They charge per wound, per bone, per illness. There is no charity healing. The Accord ensures their monopoly, and they ensure the Accord's population doesn't collapse. It is a cold, efficient arrangement.

**Salvagers & Raiders** — Drifters who run expeditions into essence-dense zones, pre-Sundering ruins, and deep mines. A single successful haul can set you up for months. Two failed ones leave you dead or destitute. This is where the players operate.

**Crucible Alchemists** — A rare breed. These individuals have learned — through obsessive experimentation — how to combine essence-infused materials in a controlled environment (the Crucible) to create new compounds and artifacts. The trade is part science, part intuition, and part madness. Most alchemists blow themselves up before they synthesize anything useful. The successful ones become wealthy and dangerously secretive about their recipes.

### Who Doesn't

**Farmers** — Agriculture exists but it's subsistence-level in most settlements. Essence contamination in soil makes large-scale farming unreliable. Crops mutate. Livestock develops tumors. Frontier folk sometimes eat things they shouldn't.

**The Old Guilds** — Traditional craftsmen (cobblers, weavers, potters) still exist but they've been economically crushed by the demand for essence-enhanced goods. Nobody wants a normal sword when an alchemically-treated blade exists.

**The Unaffiliated** — Anyone without coin, without settlement residence, without guild membership. These are the people the economy is designed to grind down. The system does not have a safety net. You produce, you fight, you trade — or you die slowly in the margins.

## The Wilderness

Beyond the walls, the world is not *empty* — it is *hostile*. Four centuries of essence saturation have reshaped the ecology.

### The Whispering Woods
Dense, ancient forest where the trees themselves have become semi-aware. Their root networks channel essence across vast distances. The wood is extraordinarily valuable — essence-infused Oak and Spruce are critical for construction and alchemy. But the forest produces predators: wolves with crystalline teeth, boar-like creatures with stone-fused hides, and worse things that move between the trees at night. Herbalists prize the Red Herbs that grow in clearings here — the essence saturation gives them genuine medicinal properties.

### The Deep Mines
Pre-Sundering mining operations that have been reclaimed by the earth. The tunnels go deep — far deeper than any modern settlement would dare to dig. The ore down there (Iron, Copper, and occasionally Rubies) carries intense essence charges. The deeper you go, the better the material, and the higher the risk of encountering cave-adapted creatures, structural collapses, and raw essence pockets that can Grey you in hours.

---

## What People Want

This is a world driven by **material desperation masquerading as ambition**. Every person in Ashenmoor, from the walled merchant to the frontier drifter, is ultimately chasing the same thing: **security**. But security costs coin. Coin requires materials. Materials require risking your life.

This is why the economy is a machine built on greed. Not cartoon villainy — practical, survival-grade greed:
- You enter the Deep Mines because the iron there sells for 10x surface price.
- You gamble on the Crucible because a single discovered recipe could make you rich enough to buy a permanent residence inside the walls.
- You hire a Therapist instead of letting your wounds fester because the alternative is infection, then Greying, then death.
- You hoard materials because you've learned that empty hands mean empty stomachs.

Nobody is evil. Everybody is desperate. And the world keeps demanding more.
