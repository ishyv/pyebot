# Factions & Power Structures

## The Merchant's Accord

The de facto governing body of civilized Ashenmoor. Not a government in any traditional sense — there are no elections, no constitutions, no rights. The Accord is a **trade cartel** that became too important to fail.

### Structure
- **The Accord Council**: Seven senior merchants from the largest walled settlements. They set coin minting rates, tariff schedules, and trade route licensing. Decisions are made by simple majority vote. Bribery is standard operating procedure.
- **Garrison Captains**: Each walled settlement maintains its own Garrison, funded by Accord taxes. The Captains answer to the Council but retain operational independence. Some are competent soldiers. Some are political appointees.
- **Licensed Operators**: Therapists, Smiths, Crucible Alchemists, and other specialists operate under Accord licenses. The fee is substantial and annual. Operating without a license is punishable by exile.

### Philosophy
The Accord does not care about justice. It cares about *stability* — because stability means trade, and trade means wealth. They will tolerate corruption, exploitation, and inequality as long as the coin flows and the walls stand. They crack down hard on two things: counterfeiting and unlicensed alchemy (the latter because unregulated Crucible work has historically destroyed entire settlements).

## The Therapists' Circle

A professional guild that holds a near-total monopoly on healing. The Accord granted them this monopoly generations ago in exchange for guaranteed service to Garrison soldiers. Every walled settlement has at least one licensed Therapist clinic.

### How It Works
- Healing is **transactional**. You pay per unit of treatment. No credit. No charity.
- Therapists use essence-derived salves, splints, and surgical techniques. This is not mystical healing — it is medicine enhanced by essence-infused herbs and compounds.
- The Circle internally shares medical knowledge but guards it fiercely from outsiders. Unlicensed healing is prosecuted.
- Frontier settlements rarely have Therapists. Out there, you stitch yourself up or you find someone who learned from a Therapist and operates illegally.

### The Moral Reality
Most Therapists are not cruel. They charge because the Accord demands license fees, because their materials are expensive, and because demand always exceeds supply. Some of them genuinely hate the system. They still charge, because the alternative is losing their license and their ability to help anyone at all.

## The Crucible Lodges

Loose, secretive associations of alchemists who practice essence synthesis. There is no single Lodge — different settlements have different traditions, different knowledge bases, and fierce rivalries.

### The Craft
Crucible alchemy is the practice of combining essence-infused raw materials to create new compounds, tools, and artifacts. It is equal parts chemistry, intuition, and luck. The Crucible itself is a reinforced stone vessel designed to contain the volatile reactions that occur when multiple essence-charged materials interact.

### The Culture
- **Recipe Secrecy**: A successful recipe is worth a fortune. Alchemists guard their discoveries with paranoia bordering on psychosis. Some encode their notes. Some memorize everything and write nothing down. Some simply lie about their ingredients.
- **Community Discovery**: Despite the secrecy, the alchemist community is driven by a perverse form of collective science. Someone discovers that Oak Wood + Red Herb + Ruby produces something remarkable, and within weeks, word spreads through tavern whispers and coded messages. The *discoverer* gets prestige. Everyone else gets to try to replicate it.
- **Volatile Failures**: Bad combinations don't just fail — they can detonate, release toxic fumes, or produce cursed substances (Volatile Ash being the most common benign failure). This is why the Accord regulates the practice. An uncontrolled Crucible explosion took out half a settlement's market district forty years ago. People remember.

## The Frontier Guilds

Unlike the formal Accord structures, the frontier operates on informal guilds organized by trade:

### Miners
Organized around specific mine sites. A mining camp is led by whoever found the vein first or whoever is strongest enough to hold it. Miners are tough, practical people who speak in terms of yield rates and tunnel stability. They pull Stone, Copper Ore, Iron Ore, and occasionally Rubies from the earth. A good miner can become wealthy. A careless miner becomes a cautionary tale.

### Loggers
Timber crews operate at the edges of the Whispering Woods. They work in teams because solo logging is suicide — the forest fauna will take a lone individual within hours. Loggers harvest Oak and Spruce primarily, with rare Palm Wood available from the southeastern coastal forests. The work is grueling and monotonous, punctuated by moments of extreme violence when something in the trees decides the crew is prey.

### Herbalists
The quietest and perhaps strangest of the frontier professions. Herbalists venture into essence-saturated zones to gather Red Herbs, Wolf Teeth (sold to alchemists for their organic-sharpness trait combination), and other biological materials. They tend to be solitary, patient, and slightly unhinged from long exposure to low-level essence fields.

## The Drifters (Player Characters)

Drifters are the system's wild cards. They have no settlement loyalty, no guild obligations, and no safety net. They survive by running expeditions into dangerous zones to extract valuable materials, which they sell to settlement merchants or use in their own Crucible experiments.

### Why Someone Becomes a Drifter
- **Exile**: Broke a law, defaulted on a debt, crossed the wrong merchant.
- **Ambition**: Decided the ceiling of settlement life was too low. Wanted wealth on their own terms.
- **Desperation**: Had nothing left inside the walls. The wilderness at least offers a chance.
- **Curiosity**: Heard about pre-Sundering ruins, about essence anomalies, about things in the deep that don't match anything in the known bestiary. Went to look. Never came back to normal life.

### The Drifter Economy
Drifters operate on a brutal cycle:
1. **Spend coin** to heal, equip, and upgrade their stash.
2. **Risk their lives** on expeditions to gather materials.
3. **Extract** (or die trying) and **sell or craft** what they found.
4. **Repeat** — but now with slightly better gear, slightly more knowledge, and slightly worse odds (because the easy zones are already picked clean).

The system is designed to eat them alive. Most Drifters break even at best. The exceptional ones — those who discover rare Crucible recipes, who find deep-vein Rubies, who survive where others don't — become legends. For a while. Until the next expedition.
